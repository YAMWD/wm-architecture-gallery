How to read a card

Each card is read from the bottom up: the encoder turns observations into the sequence, the token row shows what one step of the sequence contains, the transformer box lists its blocks (attention masks, where actions enter), the sampling box says how the next state is produced and how many passes that costs, and the decoder turns the prediction back into observations. The dashed orange arrow is the autoregressive or planning loop.

Scope: models whose dynamics model or generative backbone is a transformer. U-Net diffusion models, RNN world models and MLP latent models are out of scope. Cards mark their evidence level: verified facts survived a 3-vote adversarial check, read facts were read from the paper or code, claim facts come from a developer page only. Numbers marked [D] are our own arithmetic.

Maintained in the open: send corrections and new cards as issues or pull requests (see CONTRIBUTING in the repository). Every card has a permalink and a downloadable SVG.

Trajectory Transformer

2021 · UC Berkeley · size not disclosed (~0.8M [D])
RL world models (small, latent) Discrete-token model read code + weights

NeurIPS 2021 · arXiv · code · page

Offline RL as one big sequence-modeling problem: every state dimension, action dimension, the reward and the reward-to-go are discretized into 100 bins and become separate tokens of a small GPT. There is no learned encoder and no policy network; acting is beam search over the model's own trajectory predictions, extended one token at a time. Latency scales with state dimensionality, and the authors note it can take seconds per action, which rules out real-time control.

RepresentationEach of the N state and M action dimensions, the reward and the reward-to-go is one token: per-dimension uniform or quantile discretization into V = 100 bins; input vocabulary V × (N + M + 2), output logits over V
CoreGPT-style decoder: 4 layers, 4 heads, token embedding d 128, dropout 0.1; parameter count not disclosed (~12·4·128² ≈ 0.8M block weights if the MLP is 4× wide [D])
AttentionCausal over all tokens; context 5 transitions of (s, a, r, R) = 5 × (N + M + 2) tokens, e.g. 125 tokens for a 17-D state and 6-D action [D]
ActionsAction dimensions are ordinary tokens in the stream, decoded after the state tokens of the same step
PredictionNext-token softmax, one token per pass, teacher-forced training; planning = beam search, width 256, horizon 15 transitions, observations decoded greedily (k_obs 1), actions sampled from the top 20 tokens; offline RL ranks transitions by reward plus reward-to-go instead of log-probability
DecoderNone; states are discretized scalars, decoded back by bin centres
MemoryFixed context of 5 transitions; no KV-cache details given
Speed'Up to multiple seconds for action selection when the context window grows too large', hardware not stated (authors, Sec. 6); training 6-12 h per model on one V100 for 80 epochs (authors, App. A)
Key detailPer executed action: 15 × (N + M + 2) sequential single-token decodes with the 256 beams batched, e.g. 375 for HalfCheetah [D]; a latency-bound sub-1M-parameter workload

Sources: arXiv 2106.02039 Sec. 3.1 (tokenization, 4 layers/4 heads), Sec. 3.2 (beam search, reward-to-go), Algorithm 1, Sec. 6 (seconds per action); arXiv 2106.02039 App. A (d 128, dropout 0.1, V100 6-12 h), App. E (beam width 256, horizon 15, V 100, context 5, k_obs, k_act); Environment dimensions for the [D] examples: D4RL HalfCheetah (17-D state, 6-D action)

TransDreamer

2022 · Rutgers / KAIST · size not disclosed
RL world models (small, latent) Per-step latent model read closed

arXiv preprint (v2 Nov 2024) · arXiv

The first transformer world model in the Dreamer setting. The Transformer State-Space Model (TSSM) replaces the RSSM's GRU: the deterministic state h_t is a causal transformer over the past stochastic latents and actions, one token per step, and the posterior drops h_t so every step trains in parallel. Everything else is DreamerV2: CNN encoder and decoder, discrete latents, reward and discount heads, actor-critic in imagination. The transformer is tiny (2 layers), so imagination is latency-bound per step and attention over the growing imagined history is the only cost that scales.

RepresentationDreamerV2 CNN encoder; posterior q(z_t | x_t) computed from the frame alone (no h_t), discrete stochastic latent; full state is the concatenation [h_t, z_t]
CoreTSSM: 2-layer transformer, 10 heads, attention and MLP hidden size 200 (DMC) or 600 (Atari), no dropout, no gating, no identity-map reordering (except Atari Pong); ~0.5M (DMC) to ~4.3M (Atari) block weights [D]
AttentionCausal over past steps: h_t = transformer(z_{1:t-1}, a_{1:t-1}); relative positional embedding (Transformer-XL style) is a tunable option; sequence length per DreamerV2 config
ActionsThe action is part of each step's input token together with the stochastic latent
PredictionPrior p(z_t | h_t) sampled and fed back; 1 transformer pass per imagined step; image, reward and discount predictors read (h_t, z_t); ELBO with KL(q(z_t|x_t) || p(z_t|h_t))
DecoderDreamerV2 CNN decoder from (h_t, z_t), used for the reconstruction loss and visualisation only
MemoryAttends over the whole (z, a) history of the training sequence or imagined rollout; no KV cache mentioned; transformer memory forces only K = 3 imagined trajectories per replay sample (DMC, Atari)
SpeedNo wall-clock or throughput figure; hardware not stated. On DMC and Atari it reaches returns comparable to Dreamer but is 'slower to saturate in general' (authors, Sec. 5.5); on Crafter it imagines until the trajectory's max step (App.)
Key detailOne token per step and a 2-layer core: the template for TWM, STORM and UniZero; the parallel-training trick (posterior without h_t) is what makes the transformer usable here

Sources: arXiv 2202.09481 Sec. 3.1 and Table 1 (TSSM components), Sec. 3.2 (prioritized replay, K imagined trajectories); arXiv 2202.09481 App. A.3 (2 layers, 10 heads, hidden 200/600, no gating/dropout, K = 3), Fig. 6 (rollout); DreamerV2 config referenced in App. A.3 (github.com/danijar/dreamerv2) for encoder/decoder and latent type

IRIS

2022 · University of Geneva · ~30M
RL world models (small, latent) Discrete-token model verified code + weights

ICLR 2023 (oral, notable top 5%) · arXiv · code

The archetype of the token-level world model for reinforcement learning. A VQ-VAE turns each 64x64 Atari frame into 16 discrete codes, and a small GPT predicts the next frame's codes one token at a time, together with reward and termination. The agent is trained entirely inside this imagination. Each imagined frame costs 17 dependent transformer passes, which is the cost later designs attack.

Representation16 VQ codes per 64x64 frame, vocabulary 512 (VQGAN autoencoder without the discriminator)
CoreGPT-2-style causal transformer: 10 layers, d 256, 4 heads (minGPT)
AttentionCausal over all tokens; context 20 steps = 340 tokens; KV cache reset at 340 tokens
ActionsOne action token between frames
PredictionNext-token softmax; 17 single-token passes per frame (1 action + 16 codes)
DecoderVQ-VAE CNN decoder; the actor-critic reads decoded frames, so it runs during imagination
MemoryFull causal context, ~3.5 MB of KV per sequence in fp16 [D]
SpeedNo inference figure reported; ~3.5 A100-days of training per game (authors)
Key detailMean human-normalized score 1.046 on Atari 100k; 17 serial passes per frame

Sources: arXiv 2209.00588 Tables 3-4, 6; github.com/eloialonso/iris world_model_env.py (17 passes); DIAMOND arXiv 2405.12399 (30M count)

SlotFormer

2022 · University of Toronto · size not disclosed
Object-centric world models Object-centric model read code + weights

ICLR 2023 · arXiv · code

The reference object-centric dynamics model. A frozen SAVi (or STEVE) encoder turns each frame into a handful of object slots, and a small transformer encoder over the slots of the last T frames predicts all of the next frame's slots in one pass, then rolls out autoregressively. There are no actions; rollouts feed video prediction, VQA and PHYRE planning. The transformer sees only tens of tokens per step, so the encoder and the per-slot spatial-broadcast decoder carry most of the compute.

RepresentationN object slots per frame from a frozen SAVi (STEVE on Physion): 6-8 slots of 128-d (192-d on Physion) at 64×64 or 128×128; a linear layer maps slots to d_e = 128 (OBJ3D) or 256
CoreBERT-style Pre-LN transformer encoder: N_T = 4 layers on OBJ3D and CLEVRER, 8 on Physion and PHYRE; 8 heads; FFN 4×d_e; parameter count not disclosed (~3M block weights for 4 layers at d 256 [D])
AttentionFull bidirectional self-attention over the T×N slot tokens of the history; temporal-only sinusoidal positional encoding so slots of one frame share it (permutation equivariant); T = 6 burn-in frames on OBJ3D and CLEVRER (6 × 7 = 42 tokens on CLEVRER [D]), 15 on Physion, 1 on PHYRE
ActionsNone; unconditional video prediction, with VQA and PHYRE task-success readouts on the rollouts
PredictionOne pass predicts all N next-frame slots jointly: the last N outputs go through a linear layer plus a residual from the current slots, L2 slot loss (+ image loss); K = 10 rollout frames, predictions fed back
DecoderFrozen SAVi spatial-broadcast CNN decoder (16×16 broadcast grid) renders each slot as RGB plus alpha mask; STEVE's transformer decoder on Physion
MemorySliding history of T frames = T×N tokens; no KV cache (bidirectional encoder)
SpeedNo inference figure; 'can be trained on servers with 4 modern GPUs in less than 5 days' (authors)
Key detail~42 tokens per step through a 4-layer encoder: the attention is negligible, the SAVi encoder and per-slot decoder dominate

Sources: arXiv 2210.05861 Sec. 3.2-3.3 (architecture, training), Sec. 4.5 ablations (N_T, T, K, P.E.); arXiv 2210.05861 App. C Table 8 (per-dataset N, D_slot, D_e, N_T, T, K), App. C text (8 heads, FFN 4×, Pre-LN, 16×16 broadcast); Reproducibility statement (4 GPUs, < 5 days); github.com/pairlab/SlotFormer (code and weights)

TWM

2023 · TU Dortmund · 19M (10.8M dynamics)
RL world models (small, latent) Per-step latent model read code

ICLR 2023 · arXiv · code

A Dreamer-style agent whose dynamics model is a causal Transformer-XL instead of a GRU. A CNN VAE turns each 64x64 observation into 32x32 categorical latents; latent, action and reward enter as three separate tokens per step, and the hidden state read from the action token drives MLP heads for the next latent, reward and discount. The policy acts from z_t alone, so the transformer runs only in training and imagination, where the XL memory doubles throughput to 39,000 samples/s on one A100.

Representation32 categorical variables x 32 classes per step from a CNN VAE on 64x64 grayscale frames (stack of 4)
CoreTransformer-XL: 10 layers, d 256, 4 heads of 64, FFN 1024, SiLU; dynamics model 10.8M, world model 19M (Table 5)
AttentionCausal with relative positions; history 16 steps = 47 tokens (3x16-1) [D], plus Transformer-XL segment memory
ActionsOwn action token through a modality-specific linear embedding; h_t is read from the action token's output
PredictionOne pass per imagined step; MLP heads give the next latent (categorical sample), reward (mean) and discount
DecoderCNN decoder trains the VAE only; the policy is conditioned on z_t, so nothing is decoded in imagination
MemoryCached XL segment outputs: imagination 39,000 samples/s with the memory vs 19,900 with a vanilla transformer (~2x) [D]
SpeedImagination 39,000 samples/s on one A100 at batch 400 and ~10 h per Atari run (authors); policy 653 frames/s on a CPU
Key detail3 tokens per step, against 1 in STORM and 17 in IRIS; the transformer is never needed at deployment

Sources: arXiv 2303.07109 Sec. 2.1 (tokens, Transformer-XL), Table 4 (hyperparameters), Table 5 (parameters), App. A.1 (wall-clock); verified_findings Finding 3 (STORM Table 1: TWM 3 tokens per step, 10 layers)

RAP (Reasoning via Planning)

2023 · UC San Diego · 33B (LLaMA-33B, frozen)
LLM and code world models LLM or mixture of transformers read code

EMNLP 2023 · arXiv · code

The template for using an off-the-shelf LLM as a world model. A frozen LLaMA-33B is prompted three ways: as the policy that proposes actions, as the world model that writes the next state in natural language, and as the reward model. Monte Carlo tree search runs 10-20 iterations over these text states before one answer is committed. Every tree expansion is a full LLM generation, so inference is serial 33B decoding inside a search loop, with no training at all.

RepresentationA state is natural-language text: the block configuration (Blocksworld), the values of intermediate variables (GSM8k) or the current fact (PrOntoQA); an action is a text step (a block move, a sub-question, a rule)
CoreLLaMA-33B used frozen with prompts (60 layers, d 6656, 52 heads, 32.5B; LLaMA paper Table 2); the same model serves as policy, world model and reward model; Llama-2 70B in the full-Blocksworld experiment
AttentionOrdinary causal LLM attention over prompt (in-context demonstrations + state) and generated text; each generation is cut at 2,048 tokens or a newline
ActionsText in the prompt: the world model is queried with (state, action) and writes the next state; the policy samples d candidate actions per expansion
PredictionSampled next-token decoding at temperature 0.8; one LLM generation per state transition; rewards from action log-probability, answer confidence (majority over several samples) and the probability of 'Yes' to 'Is this reasoning step correct?'
DecoderNone; states are text
MemoryThe MCTS tree stores text states and Q-values; reward prompts hide the history and feed only the current state
SpeedNo latency figure; all experiments on 4× NVIDIA A5000 24 GB (authors). Per iteration: d expansions × (action proposal + next-state generation + reward queries) plus a roll-out to a terminal state, each a 33B decode [D: structural, from Algorithm 1]
Key detailRAP(20) with LLaMA-33B solves 42% of 6-step Blocksworld tasks vs 40% for GPT-4 CoT; cost grows with iterations × depth × branching and is bound by memory-bandwidth-limited decoding

Sources: arXiv 2305.14992 Sec. 3.1-3.3 (world model, rewards, MCTS), Sec. 4.1 (LLaMA-33B, T 0.8), Table 1; arXiv 2305.14992 App. A (MCTS changes), App. B.1-B.2 (decoding limit, 4× A5000), Algorithm 1; LLaMA arXiv 2302.13971 Table 2 (33B config, fetched from ar5iv to fill the layer/width/head numbers)

GAIA-1

2023 · Wayve · 6.5B (+2.6B video decoder)
Discrete-token video models Discrete-token model read closed

company technical report · arXiv

A generative world model for driving: a 6.5B causal transformer predicts the next image token over an interleaved sequence of text, image and action tokens, trained from scratch on 4,700 h of London driving. Each 288x512 frame is 576 discrete tokens from a 0.3B DINO-distilled tokenizer, so one frame costs 576 serial passes, and a separate 2.6B video diffusion U-Net, not a transformer, turns the tokens into pixels with 50 DDIM steps. The report says the model does not yet run in real time.

Representation576 discrete tokens per 288x512 frame (18x32, downsampling 16), vocabulary 8,192, from a 0.3B image tokenizer trained with a DINO distillation loss
Core6.5B decoder-only transformer trained from scratch; layer, width and head counts not disclosed
AttentionDense causal attention over 26 frames at 6.25 Hz (4 s): 26 x (32 text + 576 image + 2 action) = 15,860 tokens; FlashAttention v2; sliding window beyond the context
Actions2 action tokens and 32 T5-large text tokens per step in the interleaved sequence; conditioning tokens are dropped at random in training, giving unconditional, action- and text-conditioned generation
PredictionNext-token softmax with top-k = 50: 'n forward steps to generate one new image frame', so 576 serial passes per frame; optional classifier-free guidance doubles the evaluations [D]
Decoder2.6B video diffusion 3D U-Net with factorized space/time attention: 50 DDIM steps per window of 7 frames (2 overlapping context frames), then temporal upsampling 6.25 → 12.5 → 25 Hz
MemoryKV-cache policy not disclosed; context capped at 26 frames with a sliding window for longer rollouts
SpeedNot disclosed; generation 'does not yet run at real-time' (authors). Training: world model 15 days on 64 A100-80GB, tokenizer 4 days on 32, decoder 15 days on 32 = ~1,568 A100-days [D]
Key detail576 dependent decode steps per frame through 6.5B parameters, at least 13 GB of bf16 weights streamed per step [D], followed by a 50-step diffusion decoder: a bandwidth-bound workload

Sources: arXiv 2309.17080 Sec. 2.1-2.4 (tokens, world model, decoder), Sec. 4.1-4.3 (sizes, training), Sec. 5.1-5.2 (sampling, DDIM), Sec. 8 (not real time); ar_token notes Group 1 (GAIA-1) for the [D] arithmetic

STORM

2023 · Beijing Institute of Technology · ~16.5M (4.2M in blocks)
RL world models (small, latent) Per-step latent model verified code

NeurIPS 2023 · arXiv · code

The transformer world model cut down to one token per environment step. A CNN categorical VAE encodes each 64x64 frame as 32x32 categoricals, an MLP action mixer fuses that code with the action into a single d 512 token, and a 2-layer GPT predicts the next latent distribution, reward and continuation. Imagination draws one categorical sample per step at batch 1024 and decodes no pixels, so a full Atari 100k run takes 4.3 h on one RTX 3090.

Representation32 categoricals x 32 classes per 3x64x64 frame from a CNN categorical VAE
CoreGPT-style causal transformer: 2 layers, d 512, 8 heads, FFN 1024, post-LN, learned absolute positions; ~4.2M block parameters, ~16.5M world model (verifier count from the code)
AttentionCausal over one token per step; training batch 16 x 64 steps; imagination 8 context + 16 imagined steps
ActionsAction mixer MLP (Linear-LN-ReLU-Linear-LN) fuses z_t and a_t into one d 512 token before the transformer
PredictionOne pass per imagined step; one categorical sample from the predicted next distribution; no iterative decoding
DecoderCNN decoder trains the VAE only; the agent acts on [z_t, h_t], so no pixels are decoded in imagination
Memory24 cached steps at batch 1024: ~50 MB bf16 as implemented (the released 'KV cache' stores layer inputs and recomputes K and V each step), ~100 MB as a true K/V cache (verifier arithmetic)
Speed4.3 h per Atari 100k run on one RTX 3090 = 9.3 V100-hours at 11.9 training FPS (authors); no imagination throughput reported
Key detail1 token per step and 2 layers: a transformer whose weights and cache fit in tens of MB, but per-step cost grows with context because K and V are recomputed

Sources: verified_findings Finding 3 (arXiv 2310.09615 Tables 1, 5-10, 12; github.com/weipu-zhang/STORM); arXiv 2310.09615 Sec. 3.1-3.2 (action mixer, KV cache, agent state [z_t, h_t])

Genie

2024 · Google DeepMind · 10.1B dynamics (10.7B total)
Discrete-token video models Discrete-token model read closed

ICML 2024 · arXiv

A foundation world model trained on 30,000 h of unlabelled 2D-platformer video. An ST-ViViT video tokenizer (200M) turns 160x90 frames into discrete codes, a latent action model (300M) infers one of 8 latent actions between frames, and a 10.1B decoder-only MaskGIT dynamics transformer predicts the next frame's tokens from past frames and actions. Each ST block runs spatial attention over one frame, causal temporal attention over the 16-frame window, then an FFW; a frame costs 25 MaskGIT steps, and the authors report about 1 FPS.

RepresentationDiscrete codes from a 200M ST-ViViT VQ-VAE (patch 4, 1,024 codes x 32-d) on 160x90 frames at 10 FPS; the token grid is not stated, ~920 tokens per frame implied by the stated token counts [D]
CoreMaskGIT dynamics transformer: 48 layers, 36 heads, d 5120, k/q size 128; 10.1B (10.7B with tokenizer and LAM, rounded to '11B' in the abstract)
AttentionST-transformer block: spatial self-attention over one frame's tokens, causal temporal attention over the same position across 16 frames, then FFW; the spatial term scales linearly with frames
Actions8 discrete latent actions from a 300M latent action model (patch 16, VQ codebook of 8) learned without action labels; actions enter as additive embeddings on the frame tokens
PredictionMaskGIT: 25 steps per frame at temperature 2 with random sampling, all tokens of a frame per pass; frames generated autoregressively
DecoderST-ViViT tokenizer decoder (20 layers, d 1024, 16 heads) → 160x90 frames
Memory16-frame window, which the authors call a limitation; KV policy not disclosed
Speed'Currently operates around 1 FPS', hardware not stated (authors). Training: 125k steps at batch 512 on 256 TPUv5p, 6.6e22 FLOPs over 942B tokens
Key detail25 parallel passes over ~920 tokens per frame through 10.1B parameters, roughly 0.46 PFLOP per frame [D]: compute-bound, the opposite profile to GAIA-1's 576 serial steps

Sources: arXiv 2402.15391 Sec. 2.1-2.2 (ST-transformer, LAM, dynamics), Sec. 3 (training details, 25 MaskGIT steps), Sec. 6 (1 FPS), App. C Tables 5, 7, 10; arxiv.org/html/2402.15391 Table 12 (48 layers, 36 heads, d 5120; fetched because the local text lacks it); ar_token notes Group 1 (Genie) for the ~920-token and 0.46 PFLOP arithmetic

REM

2024 · Technion · size not disclosed (~4M [D])
RL world models (small, latent) Discrete-token model read code

ICML 2024 · arXiv · code

An IRIS-style token world model whose transformer is replaced by a Retentive Network and whose per-token decode loop is removed. Retention keeps a fixed-size recurrent state instead of a KV cache, and Parallel Observation Prediction (POP) feeds 64 learned prediction tokens from that state to sample all 64 next-frame codes in one call. Imagination becomes 2 calls per frame instead of 64, which the authors measure as 15.4× faster than prior token-based world models.

Representation64 VQ codes per 64×64 frame (8×8 grid), vocabulary 512; VQ-GAN-style CNN tokenizer without the discriminator (IRIS uses 16 codes)
CoreRetNet (retention, not softmax attention): 5 layers, 4 retention heads, d 256, FFN 1024, dropout 0.1; parameter count not disclosed (~3.9M block weights [D])
AttentionMulti-scale retention with a per-head exponential decay and relative position; chunkwise mode is parallel inside a chunk of c = 3 observation-action blocks (195 tokens [D]) and recurrent between chunks through a d×d state per head; world-model context 2 frames
ActionsOne action token per step (learned embedding) after the 64 observation tokens
PredictionPOP: K = 64 learned prediction tokens u are run from the recurrent state S_t and all next-frame tokens are sampled in one pass; default mode = 2 sequential calls per imagined frame (state update with z_t, a_t, then prediction with u), so 2H calls over the horizon H = 10 instead of KH = 640 (Table 6)
DecoderVQ-VAE CNN decoder for the reconstruction loss only; the actor-critic reads the token code vectors through a small CNN + LSTM, so imagination decodes no pixels
MemoryFixed-size retention state per layer and head replaces a growing KV cache; u tokens never enter the state update
Speed15.4× faster imagination than prior token-based world models and under 12 h of training per game (authors); run times measured on an RTX 4090 workstation, other experiments on V100s
Key detailPOP doubles the tokens per observation prediction (2K vs K, Table 6) but turns KH sequential calls into 2H; superhuman on 12 of 26 Atari 100k games (authors)

Sources: arXiv 2402.05643 Sec. 2.2-2.4 (retention, POP, training), Fig. 4, Sec. 4 (K = 64, RTX 4090 / V100), abstract (15.4×, < 12 h); arXiv 2402.05643 Table 2 (H 10, N 512, 64×64), Table 5 (world-model config), Table 6 (call counts), Table 8 and A.1.3 (actor-critic input)

iVideoGPT

2024 · Tsinghua University · 138M (436M scaled)
Discrete-token video models Discrete-token model read code + weights

NeurIPS 2024 · arXiv · code · page

A GPT world model for robot manipulation with a compressive tokenizer: context frames are encoded on their own into 16x16 = 256 VQ tokens, but every future frame is encoded, conditioned on the context frames through cross-attention, into only 4x4 = 16 tokens. A LLaMA-style transformer (12 layers, d 768, 138M) predicts the sequence token by token, so a future frame costs 17 passes instead of 257, and actions enter by addition to the slot token that precedes each frame. On an RTX 4090 generation takes 1.11 s against 22.5 s with a plain 16x16 tokenizer.

RepresentationCompressive conditional VQGAN (114M at 64x64): 256 tokens per context frame, 16 tokens per future frame, codebooks of 8,192 each; transformer vocabulary 16,386 incl. 2 slot tokens
CoreLLaMA-style GPT (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE): 12 layers, d 768, 12 heads, FFN 3072, 138M; scaling run 24 layers, d 1024, 16 heads, 436M
AttentionDense causal over the interleaved clip: L = (N+1) T0 + (n+1)(T - T0) - 1 = 751 tokens for 16 frames with 2 context frames [D]
ActionsAction linearly projected and added to the slot-token embedding before each future frame; optional reward head on the last token of each frame (MSE)
PredictionNext-token softmax: 17 passes per future frame (slot + 16 tokens) against 257 with a per-frame 16x16 tokenizer [D]
DecoderConditional VQGAN decoder with cross-attention to the context-frame features → 64x64 (a 256x256 variant uses a 310M VQGAN)
MemoryKV policy not disclosed; the whole 12-16-frame clip stays in context
SpeedRTX 4090, batch 1: tokenize 0.29 s, generation 1.11 s, detokenize 0.06 s, 2.33 GB; 22.5 s generation with a 16x16-per-frame tokenizer (authors, Table 8; rollout length not stated in the caption)
Key detailTokens per predicted frame, 16 against 256, is the lever: 20x faster generation [D] at 138M, where the number of passes, not FLOPs, sets the latency

Sources: arXiv 2405.15223 Sec. 3.1-3.3 (tokenizer, transformer, action conditioning), Sec. 4.5 (scaling), App. Table 2 (architecture), Table 3 (sequence lengths), Table 8 (RTX 4090 timings)

UniZero

2024 · Shanghai AI Laboratory · size not disclosed
RL world models (small, latent) Per-step latent model read code

arXiv preprint · arXiv · code

A MuZero-style planner whose dynamics network is a 2-layer transformer over two tokens per step, latent and action. A conv encoder maps each observation to a 768-d SimNorm latent with no decoder, and the transformer output feeds heads for the next latent, reward, policy and value. Every real action runs 50 MCTS simulations, each a single-step transformer call against a KV cache of the last 4 steps, so inference is a small-batch, latency-bound loop rather than a FLOP-bound one.

RepresentationConv encoder → one 768-d latent per step with SimNorm (simplex groups of 8); 64x64 Atari frames; no reconstruction
Core2 transformer layers, d 768, 8 heads, GELU, dropout 0.1 (Atari, DMC); d 64 and 4 heads for the long-term memory tasks; FFN width and parameter count not listed
AttentionCausal over interleaved (latent, action) tokens; training context H = 10 steps = 20 tokens; inference context H_infer = 4 steps (memory_length + 16 on long-term tasks)
ActionsA learned action-embedding token per step (lookup table for discrete actions, 2-layer MLP for continuous ones)
PredictionOne pass per imagined step: the dynamics head regresses the next latent (SimNorm) and reward, the decision head gives policy and value
DecoderNone: no reconstruction loss; a decode-regularization ablation did not help
MemoryKV cache over the last H_infer = 4 steps = 8 tokens [D], reused by every node of the search tree
SpeedAtari 100k agent trained in ~4 h on one A100 80GB, 1M VisualMatch steps in ~30 h (authors); no inference latency reported
Key detail50 MCTS simulations per real step = 50 dependent single-step transformer calls on an 8-token cache: a latency-bound inner loop

Sources: arXiv 2406.10667 Sec. 3.2-3.3 (tokens, KV cache, MCTS), App. B Tables 6-8 (architecture, hyperparameters), App. B.5 (compute)

Δ-IRIS

2024 · University of Geneva · 25M
RL world models (small, latent) Discrete-token model verified code + weights

ICML 2024 · arXiv · code

IRIS with context-aware tokens. A CNN autoencoder conditioned on the previous frame and action encodes only the stochastic change between steps as 4 delta tokens from a 1,024-entry vocabulary, and a 3-layer GPT sees 6 tokens per step instead of IRIS's 17: one continuous I-token (a frame embedding from an auxiliary CNN), one action token and the 4 delta tokens. At 25M parameters it collects 20 Crafter frames per second of training on one A100, 10x IRIS-64, while still paying 4 dependent passes and a decoder call per imagined frame.

Representation4 delta tokens per 64x64 frame, vocabulary 1,024, from a CNN autoencoder conditioned on the previous frame and action; plus 1 continuous I-token per step from an auxiliary CNN
CoreGPT-like causal transformer: 3 layers, d 512, 8 heads; 25M parameters for the whole agent (Table 1)

SANA-WM

2026 · NVIDIA · 2.6B (+17B optional refiner)
State-space and hybrid designs SSM hybrid read code + weights

arXiv preprint · arXiv

A minute-scale camera-controlled world model whose DiT replaces most softmax attention with frame-wise Gated DeltaNet: 15 of 20 blocks keep a D×D recurrent state per head, updated once per latent frame, and only 5 blocks use softmax attention, run at deployment with an attention sink plus a local window. The LTX2 VAE packs 8 raw frames into one 128-channel latent frame, so 60 s of 720p fits one GPU. Self Forcing distillation gives a 4-step chunk-causal generator; with NVFP4 on one RTX 5090 it denoises 60 s of 720p in 34 s (authors).

RepresentationLTX2 VAE, 128 latent channels, one latent frame per 8 raw frames; spatial stride and tokens per latent frame at 720p are not stated in the paper, which calls the representation 8x smaller than Wan2.1-VAE's
CoreHybrid linear DiT from SANA-Video: 20 blocks, d 2240, 20 heads of 112; 15 frame-wise Gated DeltaNet blocks + softmax attention at layers {3, 7, 11, 15, 19}, keeping the original QKV and output projections; text, video and pose tokens pass through the alternating blocks
AttentionGDN blocks: a D×D recurrent state per head updated once per latent frame from all S spatial tokens (forward scan global, reversed scan reset at chunk boundaries in the chunk-causal variant); softmax blocks: first latent frame as sink + local temporal window at deployment; chunk size not stated
Actions6-DoF camera trajectory only, no keyboard: UCPE relative camera encoding on the geometric channels of a camera attention branch at latent-frame rate, plus the 8 raw-frame Plücker raymaps of one VAE stride packed into a 48-channel tensor through a zero-initialized 3D patch embedder; applied in every block
PredictionFlow matching; 60-step chunk-causal AR generator distilled with Self Forcing to 4 steps; bidirectional, chunk-causal and distilled variants each run on one GPU
DecoderLTX2 VAE decoder → 720p at 16 fps (961 frames = 60 s [D]); optional second stage: LTX-2 17B refiner with a rank-384 LoRA, 3 Euler steps
Memory15 GDN layers hold 20 × 112² ≈ 0.25M values each, ≈7.5 MB in BF16 for all 15 [D]; the 5 softmax layers keep a sink + window KV, so memory is constant in rollout length (all-softmax runs out of memory at 60 s on an H100, Fig. 7b); no explicit 3D scene memory
Speed60 s of 720p: DiT time on one H100 21.7 min at 60 steps → 43 s at 4 steps → 29 s with the sink; 34 s end to end on one RTX 5090 with NVFP4 (authors, Fig. 7a); 433 ms per denoising step at 720p on one H100, 5.68 GiB (Table 3, 5 s clips); 24.1 one-minute videos per hour on 8 H100s (Table 2)
Key detailThe only model in this gallery whose per-frame cost is constant in rollout length in 3/4 of its layers; the high-compression VAE is the bigger lever per step (3.4x latency, 8.9 → 5.4 GiB) and the softmax layers cost 1.17x per step [D]

Cosmos 3

2026 · NVIDIA · 16B Nano (2× 8B), 64B Super
World-action models LLM or mixture of transformers read code + weights

company technical report · report · code · weights

An omnimodal world model built as a mixture of transformers: a reasoner tower (the Qwen3-VL VLM) runs autoregressively over text and ViT tokens, and a generator tower denoises video latents, audio and action tokens with flow matching. The towers keep their own norms, attention projections and MLPs but share one self-attention operator, so every diffusion token can read the AR context. One set of weights serves generation, forward and inverse dynamics and a policy, selected by which tokens are clean and which are noisy; the DROID policy runs 4 steps with CFG split across 2 RTX Pro 6000 GPUs and skips video decoding.

RepresentationGeneration: Wan2.2-TI2V-5B VAE latents, 4x temporal and 32x32 spatial (16x16 VAE + 2x2 patch merge), linearly projected to the hidden size, so (H/32) x (W/32) tokens per latent frame [D]; understanding: ViT tokens (patch 16, 2x2 merge); audio 25 tokens/s [D: 48 kHz / hop 1920]
CoreNano: 36 layers, hidden 4096, 32 heads / 8 KV heads of 128, FFN 12,288 in each of two towers = 16B (from Qwen3-VL-8B); Super: 64 layers, hidden 5120, 64 / 8 heads, FFN 25,600 = 64B; Edge 4B (28 layers, hidden 2048) not yet released
AttentionShared self-attention: AR tokens attend causally to AR tokens only; DM tokens attend bidirectionally over all AR + DM tokens; AR tokens never read DM tokens; training packs a fixed 74,000-token context
ActionsAction vectors (relative ego pose 9-D, effector pose 9-D, grasp state; 10-D single arm up to 57-D egocentric human) enter as DM tokens through per-embodiment input and output projections; the MoT backbone is shared; policy adds a fresh action encoder, decoding MLP and action embedding tokens
PredictionFlow matching: 50 steps for generation (guidance 6, shift 10), 4 steps for the DROID policy (guidance 3, shift 5) predicting 32 absolute joint-position actions at 15 Hz; the reasoner pass is computed once and cached across all steps
DecoderWan2.2 VAE decoder for video; skipped in policy serving, where only the action tokens are read
MemoryNo streaming rollout is described: the generator is bidirectional over the whole packed context; paged KV caching only for reasoner serving (TensorRT-LLM / vLLM)
SpeedNano 720p text-to-video on one GPU: 297 s on H100 NVL vs 115 s on B200 (PyTorch; frames and steps not stated); policy served on 2 RTX Pro 6000 with CFG parallelism, per-chunk latency not disclosed (company claims)
Key detailEach token passes through one tower, so per-token compute stays near the dense 8B backbone while parameters double [D]; the policy runs 4 steps x a 540x640 three-view canvas and never decodes the video latents

Matrix-Game 3.5

2026 · Skywork AI (Matrix-Game team) · 5B (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B)
Causal diffusion transformers (interactive video) Diffusion transformer read code + weights

arXiv preprint (company) · arXiv · code

Matrix-Game 3.5 returns to a fully causal generator and moves memory out of the KV cache into geometry: historical VAE-latent patches are lifted to 3D with metric depth, re-projected into the target view and inserted as tokens, so each 3-latent-frame chunk attends to a bounded pose-aware sequence of noisy targets, one clean anchor, a few context frames, patch-memory tokens and a reference prefix. Camera pose enters through tiled PRoPE with no new parameters. A 3-step causal student reaches 20 fps at 1280x704 on one H100 with INT8, GPU retrieval and a 75%-pruned decoder (company claim).

RepresentationWan2.2 VAE 4x16x16 + patchify (2x2 assumed) → 880 tokens per 22x40 latent frame at 1280x704, 2,640 per 3-latent chunk of 12 frames [D]; 48 latent channels
CoreWan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT: 30 layers, d 3072, 24 heads, FFN 14336 (HF config); the full DiT is fine-tuned with the text encoder and VAE frozen; the only learnable additions are the reference-token embeddings
AttentionBlock-causal chunks of 3 latent frames over one pose-aware sequence: noisy targets + clean anchor + context frames (1 per chunk in causal training, up to 5 in base training) + patch-memory tokens (≤1 per target cell) + a reference prefix of ≈1 frame; ≤≈7,900 tokens per chunk [D], bounded by ≈2.3x the target track; training window 1 anchor + 7 chunks = 22 latent positions
ActionsCamera pose via tiled PRoPE in every block: after RoPE, queries × Pᵀ, keys and values × P⁻¹, outputs × P, tiled over all head channels, no new parameters; translations recentred on the first target frame and log-compressed
PredictionPerceptual flow matching causal adaptation (10k steps), then curriculum self-rollout DMD (1,000 outer steps, 5:1 update ratio) → 3-step causal generator; prefix and chunk-local context held at the next noise level each substep (HiAR), anchor clean
DecoderMG-LightVAE, 75%-pruned Wan2.2 decoder → 12 frames per chunk at 1280x704; 'VAE decoding becomes the primary latency bottleneck' once the DiT is accelerated
MemoryPatch memory (MosaicMem-style): patches lifted to 3D with metric depth and poses, frustum-queried, z-buffered and scattered into a target-aligned memory frame; retrieval and dynamic-object filtering run once per segment, off the per-frame loop; the sequence does not grow with rollout length
SpeedUp to 20 output frames/s at 1280x704, batch 1, one H100, including the INT8 3-step DiT, online retrieval and MG-LightVAE decode (company claim; the VLM prompt rewriter runs asynchronously); 10.9 one-minute videos/hour at 77.0 GB peak on one H100 in the SANA-WM benchmark, undistilled model (authors' Table 1)
Key detailMemory is geometry, not cache: the per-chunk sequence is capped at ≈2.3x the target track, so DiT cost is constant in rollout length and the depth, lift and projection work sits off the per-frame critical path
